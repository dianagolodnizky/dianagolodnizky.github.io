PROBLEM STATEMENT

While the online confectionery market is growing, there is not enough emphasis on the emotional side of gifting. Current platforms offer premium treats but lack the tools for users to curate a personal unboxing moment.

ABOUT

This project was developed for a fictional business concept named Sucrée Chic. It serves as a premium online shop, a restaurant hub, and a creative gifting platform, translating the craftsmanship of a boutique patisserie into a streamlined digital space.

Users can easily buy their favourite treats or browse the full menu for the physical restaurant. This gives both online shoppers and local visitors a clear look at what the patisserie offers, making sure the experience is smooth and high-quality whether they are at home or visiting in person.

In addition to the standard shop, the website includes a special section for designing custom gift boxes. Using simple design tools and a realistic 3D preview, users can create a personal unboxing experience and send it straight to someone else. By bringing together the online store, the restaurant menu, and the gift designer, the site handles everything in one place- whether you just want a quick treat for yourself or a premium gift for a business partner.

SOLUTION

This project addresses a business need by creating an online store that extends the patisserie’s in-store experience into a refined digital space. Beyond standard e-commerce functionality, it enables customers to personalize gift packaging for the recipient, transforming a simple purchase into a meaningful, emotionally driven experience that improves usability and helps the brand stand out.

DESIGN
CONCEPT

ALL IN ONE EXPERIENCE

The visual language of the application is built around the metaphor of a bird’s nest- a secure and nurturing sanctuary. This space is designed to foster personal growth, providing users with the tools and resilience they need to eventually navigate life with confidence outside the nest.

VISUALIZING THE INVISIBLE

A core element of the design strategy is the use of transparency to represent those living with post-traumatic stress. This choice serves as a visual reflection of the lack of social recognition and understanding that often accompanies the condition. Because post-trauma is a transparent illness with no external physical markers, it is frequently overlooked or underestimated by society. The UI acknowledges this internal reality, validating the user’s experience through a sophisticated and empathetic visual language.

PRIVACY AND
PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY

The application places a high priority on user anonymity. Understanding that many survivors prefer discretion and may feel uncomfortable with public exposure, the platform is designed to be a private haven. By removing the pressure of identification, the interface creates a low-friction environment where users can engage, share, and seek help without the fear of being judged or exposed.

USER NEEDS

1. EFFORTLESS FLOW

Users need a streamlined end-to-end experience where shopping and custom design feel effortless, fast, and free of technical frustration.

2. COMMERCIAL TRANSPARENCY

Users need to see the final costs and how long it will take to make custom items so they can plan their gifts properly.

3. PRE-VISIT MENU PREVIEW

Users need to view the restaurant menu online to get to know the available dishes and plan their order before arriving at the patisserie.

4. VISUAL CONFIDENCE

Users need access to inspirations and design combinations to overcome creative blocks, ensuring their final product looks professional and premium regardless of their own artistic skills.

5. OUTCOME ASSURANCE

Users need to view a realistic 3D representation of the physical product to verify that the final result meets their expectations and guarantees their satisfaction before purchase.

6. TIMED FRESHNESS DELIVERY

Users need a seamless service where desserts are prepared and boxed on their chosen date, ensuring the gift arrives fresh and perfectly presented for the occasion.

FEATURES &
FUNCTIONALITIES

TO RESOLVE USER NEEDS

A highly structured interface with clearly defined steps ensures a fast and simple journey, from the rapid checkout for dessert selection to the guided walkthrough for package design.

An interactive 3D product preview will allow users to verify every detail of their custom creation, ensuring the final physical result meets their expectations before purchase.

Offering pre-made style choices and design templates gives users the ideas they need to create professional, luxury packaging, even if they don't have artistic skills.

An advanced scheduling will sync production with the chosen delivery date, ensuring desserts are prepared and boxed at the optimal time for maximum freshness and looking their best.

A dedicated digital menu module for dine-in will enable users to browse available dishes and familiarize themselves with the pastry shop’s offerings before their visit.

Showing real time prices and clear delivery dates helps users understand exactly what they are paying for and when their gift will arrive.

PRODUCT USER
CHALLENGES

SENSORY UNCERTAINTY

The biggest challenge in purchasing food online is the inability to smell or see the product in person. Users often worry that the dessert won't match the photos or that the texture will differ from what they imagine, creating a psychological barrier to purchase.

DESIGN MADE EASY

When designing a gift box, non-designers often feel overwhelmed by too many choices. Without clear help, they worry the final result will look messy or unprofessional. This often causes them to leave the site without finishing their purchase because they don't feel confident in their creative decisions.

FRESHNESS & LOGISTICS

Unlike shelf-stable goods, desserts have a short life. Users often worry if their order was made days ago or if it will arrive at the right time for their event. When it is not clear how the timeline from baking to delivery works, users feel stressed about the quality of the food.

SCALE MISCONCEPTION

It is difficult to judge the physical scale of a box or dessert through a screen. Users worry the package will be smaller than expected for the price, or that the items will shift and lose their presentation during transit.

COMPETITOR
ANALYSIS

LA MAISON LADURÉE

La Maison Ladurée is a luxury french patisserie that treats its desserts and packaging as high end fashion items.

FEATURES

The website includes a step-by-step tool where users first pick a box size and style from a digital gallery. Once the box is chosen, a simple grid lets them fill the open slots with their favourite macaron flavours.

SUGARFINA

Sugarfina is a modern candy shop that uses a simple grid system for building gifts. The website uses a structured layout based on a fixed-slot Bento Box design.

FEATURES

The site uses a modular grid interface based on a fixed-slot Bento Box system. It features a progress tracker for box completion, real time price calculation during item addition, and a live 3D preview of the products within the selected packaging.

UNIQUE
FEATURES

A platform where customers can design custom packaging for desserts, made to fit the recipient’s personality and the event. The interface lets users create personalized gift boxes using easy design tools and includes the option to add a personal letter to the package.

A unique digital menu that shows the dishes available at the restaurant, giving users a clear preview of what to expect at the physical pastry shop.

AGE:

LOCATION:

MARITAL STATUS:

OCCUPATION

High School Teacher

USAGE SCENARIO

Sarah is hosting a family dinner and wants to serve premium desserts. She visits the website to browse the available selection and buy a variety of items that her guests will enjoy. She navigates the catalog, selects several different products across various categories, and proceeds directly to checkout. Her goal is to secure high-quality treats quickly, ensuring they arrive in time for her event.

MOTIVATIONS AND GOALS
  • She wants a straightforward shopping experience where she can find what she needs without unnecessary steps.
  • Her main focus is the quality of the food. She looks for clear product descriptions to make sure the items meet her standards.
  • She values a standard e-commerce flow that feels familiar and easy to navigate.
PREFERNCES AND BEHAVIOR
  • She uses the main navigation and filters to move through different dessert types and flavor profiles to find specific items.
  • She reads through the details of each product carefully, looking for ingredient information and serving sizes.
  • She prefers adding items to her cart and moving straight to the payment stage without any distractions or additional features.
PAIN POINTS
  • She becomes frustrated by any extra features or complex tools that slow her down when she is trying to reach the checkout.
  • She dislikes interfaces that hide products behind complex layers or marketing banners.
  • She is bothered by missing information regarding delivery times or the specific contents of an item.

  • She dislikes generic options and hates when packaging looks like a standard, low effort template.
  • She is bothered by hidden fees and prefers to see the total cost before reaching the final checkout step.

BRAND GUIDELINES

As part of the project, I developed a comprehensive brand book that defines the visual and conceptual identity of Ken Lenefesh. The book covers the entire process from research to final execution. While the brand book was physically printed, an interactive version is also available for exploration within the project file.

The brand book begins with a project introduction and a dedication to those coping with PTSD to establish a supportive foundation, followed by an in-depth research section that explores the impact of trauma, competitive analysis, and identified pain points. It then outlines the project’s vision and core values that guided the design, along with a detailed breakdown of the visual language, typography, color palette, logo design, custom icons, and print materials. Finally, the book presents the primary outcomes- the mobile application and the promotional website.
